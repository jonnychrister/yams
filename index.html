How to Play

Yams is a dice game for 2–4 players. Each turn, you're trying to score as highly as possible in one of the game's hands.

Your turn

  1. Roll all 5 dice.
  2. You get 2 rerolls. Each time, choose any of the 5 dice to hold and reroll the rest — or don't reroll at all if you're happy.
  3. After your final roll, choose one open hand to score into. It only counts if your dice qualify.
  4. If your roll doesn't get you what you wanted, you can forego a hand instead and take 0 for it — useful if you were chasing something better on a later turn.

Every turn fills one hand, whether that's a real score or a forced zero. The game ends once everyone has filled every hand on their sheet.

Special hands

  • Yams (40 pts) — all 5 dice show the same number
  • Sequence (30 pts) — five in a row: 1-2-3-4-5 or 2-3-4-5-6
  • Full house (20 pts) — three of one number and two of another
  • Hi — score the sum of your dice, aiming as high as possible
  • Lo — score the sum of your dice, but it must be lower than your Hi. You need both a Hi and a Lo filled or neither counts. Whichever one you fill first sets the limit for the other.

Numbers (Ones through Sixes)

Score = the sum of the dice showing that number. E.g. three 6s scores 18. You need at least one die of that number to use it.

In Classic, a Numbers total of 60 or more earns a +30 bonus.

Variants

The above describes Classic. Two variants change things up:

Yambs uses the same core idea with a different hand set:

  • Hi and Lo are replaced by a single Chance hand — the sum of all 5 dice, always available
  • 3 of a kind — sum of all 5 dice, if at least 3 match
  • Gulley — sum of all 5 dice, if at least 4 match
  • Small straight — any 4 dice in a row (1-2-3-4, 2-3-4-5 or 3-4-5-6); worth 10, 14 or 18 accordingly
  • Yams is worth 40 plus the number it's rolled on — a Yams of 6s scores 46
  • The Numbers bonus needs 63+ and is worth +35 instead of +30

Peril uses the exact same hands as Classic, but with two twists: before you roll, you must declare which hand you're going for — if your final dice don't qualify for it, you score 0 and your turn ends immediately, with no switching to a different hand. Peril's Numbers bonus also works differently: any Numbers total of 30 or more earns a bonus equal to that total itself — e.g. a Numbers score of 32 adds another 32 points.

Tips

  • You don't have to declare what you're chasing before you roll — you can change your mind with every reroll.
  • Sometimes the smart play is foregoing a low-value hand to protect your numbers bonus, rather than forcing a bad score into it.
